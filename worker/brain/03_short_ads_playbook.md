# Applying the craft to short ads (15 to 60 seconds, Meta, phone footage)

The notes above come from long-form documentary editing. Take the principles, not the clock. A 60-second slow intro
or minutes of dead space does not fit an ad. What carries over:

1. **Every ad gets its own look.** Never reuse one look for every video. Before choosing, ask what this footage is
   about, who is speaking, and what mood the words carry (urgent, calm, funny, proud, serious, technical). Let the
   mood choose the pace, the caption style, the headline style, the callout style, the colours and the end screen. A
   calm, serious talk and a loud, funny one must not look alike. Check the list of recent looks and pick something else.
2. **Open a loop, then close it.** A question or surprising claim at the start, answered by the end, beats stating the
   whole point up front. Use the order of segments to do it.
3. **Use silence on purpose.** A short pause (a breathing pace, or a held beat before a key line) makes the next
   line land. Fast and tight is right for energetic talk, wrong for serious talk.
4. **Break the pattern.** After two similar beats, change something: a zoom-in on the key word, a different callout
   position, a hard cut. Repetition then surprise.
5. **Overlays serve the moment.** A callout, zoom or headline appears because the words at that moment need it, not
   to fill the screen. Fewer, better-placed overlays beat constant motion. Do not cover the speaker's face.
6. **The end screen belongs to the video.** Write the closing line and button from what the video is about and what
   the viewer should do next; do not paste a generic or off-topic call to action. Vary its layout and wording.
7. **Captions are the words spoken.** Vary the style, size, position and case to fit the mood, but never change
   what was said beyond fixing mis-heard words.
8. **No slop.** Avoid the generic template: the same white box, the same yellow captions, the same card on every
   ad. If you catch yourself reaching for the default, choose something with a reason.
